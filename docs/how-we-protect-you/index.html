How we protect you and your business during a sale

A sale can put a lot at risk: your employees, your customers, your reputation and the price itself. Here is every way we keep you and the business safe from the first call to the day after closing.

1. Confidentiality from the first conversation

Your first call, your valuation and your financials stay between you and us. We do not tell anyone you are thinking about selling, and we do not list anything without your written approval.

2. Blind listings that do not give you away

When the business goes to market, the ad describes the opportunity without the name, address, photos of the storefront or any detail that would let a local reader recognize it. We describe the region, industry and financial profile. That is enough for the right buyer to raise a hand.

3. An NDA before any identifying information

No buyer learns the name of your business, sees your financials or gets the confidential information memorandum until they sign a non-disclosure agreement. The NDA also bars them from contacting your employees, customers, vendors or landlord without permission.

4. Buyer screening and financial qualification

Signing an NDA is not enough. Before a buyer gets real information, we find out who they are, what experience they bring, how much cash they have to invest and how they plan to pay for the business. We ask for proof of funds or lender pre-qualification when it matters. Tire kickers and competitors fishing for information get filtered out early, so you only spend time with people who can close.

5. Controlled release of information

Information goes out in stages. Serious, qualified buyers get the CIM. Buyers who submit a letter of intent get deeper detail. Sensitive items like customer names, employee pay and supplier pricing are held until late in diligence, and sometimes summarized instead of handed over. Documents are shared through a secure data room so we know who saw what and when.

6. Keeping employees, customers, vendors and competitors in the dark

Showings happen after hours or away from the business. Buyers are introduced as consultants or investors, not as buyers. Calls and emails go through us, not your front desk. And we plan with you when, and how, to tell key employees, usually near or after closing, so nobody quits and no customer gets nervous.

7. Keeping you focused on running the business

The fastest way to lose value during a sale is for the owner to take their eye off the business. Revenue dips, a buyer notices, and the price drops or the deal dies. We handle the marketing, the inquiries, the screening, the follow-up and the paperwork so you can keep the numbers strong all the way to closing.

8. Pricing based on a real valuation

Overpricing does damage: the listing sits, buyers assume something is wrong, and you end up cutting the price anyway. Underpricing leaves your money on the table. We price from recast earnings and real market data, and we show you the math, so the price can hold up when a buyer and their lender check it.

9. Managing due diligence

After a letter of intent, the buyer will want to verify everything. We organize the document list, keep requests reasonable and on schedule, answer what we can, and help you get ahead of issues before they turn into a price cut. Deals that drag in diligence lose momentum, so we keep this tight.

10. Structuring terms and protecting you from bad ones

The highest offer is not always the best one. We look at the full deal: cash at closing, the buyer's financing, any seller note and whether it is secured, earnouts, inventory and working capital, non-compete scope, your transition obligations and what happens if the buyer misses a payment. We push back on terms that shift too much risk onto you.

11. Working with your attorney and CPA

We are not your lawyer or your tax advisor, and we will never act like it. We work alongside them. Your attorney reviews the purchase agreement and your CPA advises on allocation and taxes before you sign. If you do not have advisors who know business sales, we can suggest some.

12. Escrow and a clean closing

Money and documents move through a neutral closing agent, escrow or attorney, not hand to hand. We coordinate the buyer's lender, the landlord's lease assignment, lien searches, payoffs, bulk sale or tax clearance steps where they apply, and the closing checklist so nothing surprises you at the table.

13. Transition planning

We spell out your transition in writing: how long, how many hours, paid or unpaid, and what you are responsible for. A clear transition protects the buyer's success, which protects any money you are still owed, and it lets you actually walk away when it is done.

Can a competitor find out my business is for sale?

Not from the listing. Listings are blind, and nobody gets identifying information until they sign an NDA and pass screening. If a competitor is a real buyer, we control what they see and when.

What happens if a buyer breaks the NDA?

The NDA gives you legal remedies, and the staged release of information limits what any buyer could misuse in the first place. Your attorney can advise on enforcement.

Do I have to meet every interested buyer?

No. You only meet buyers who have signed an NDA, been screened, and shown they can pay for the business.
